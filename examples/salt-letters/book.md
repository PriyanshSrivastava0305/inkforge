# Salt Letters

I woke up before dawn, as I always did, the darkness outside my small lighthouse quarters a familiar comfort. The sound of gulls crying overhead was a gentle wake-up call, and I stretched, feeling the stiffness in my back from a long day's work yesterday. My routine was simple: tend to the lighthouse, check the weather forecast, make sure the light was shining bright for sailors making their way through these treacherous waters.

As I dressed in the dim light, the thought of another day at the lighthouse settled over me like a mantle. It was a weight I'd grown accustomed to carrying, but one that sometimes felt crushing. Responsibilities, I told myself, trying to shake off the feeling of restlessness that had been nagging at me for weeks.

Downstairs in the lantern room, I began my daily routine with a quiet efficiency. The lighthouse's machinery hummed along beside me, and I checked the weather forecast on my small radio, listening as the announcer spoke in a calm, detached voice about the latest forecast. Nothing out of the ordinary – a high-pressure system moving through, with scattered clouds and gentle winds.

As I worked, the sun began to rise outside, casting a warm glow over the rocky shore below. I stood at the window, feeling the cool breeze on my face, and took a deep breath, letting the scent of saltwater and seaweed fill me up. It was moments like these that I felt most connected to this place – to the sea, to the land, to the lighthouse itself.

But even as I let myself get lost in the beauty of it all, a nagging sense of disconnection crept back in. It was a feeling I'd been trying to ignore for weeks now – months, maybe even years. A sense that there was something missing, something I couldn't quite put my finger on.

I pushed the thought aside and turned back to my tasks, focusing on the maintenance schedule for the light's optics, running my hand over the polished brass surface of the reflectors. It was a soothing ritual, one that required precision and attention to detail – exactly the kind of thing I excelled at.

But as I worked, the feeling lingered, like a shadow cast across the floor. It wasn't just the lighthouse itself, or my life here on this isolated coast – it was something deeper. A sense of disconnection from... what? My past, perhaps? My family? The sea itself?

I stopped in my tracks, my hand hovering over the reflectors. Why did I feel so lost all of a sudden? It wasn't like me to doubt myself or question my choices.

As I stood there, indecision swirling inside me, I heard a faint creaking sound from downstairs. I frowned – that was odd. The lighthouse was supposed to be sealed tight, and I'd checked the windows and doors before bed last night. It couldn't be the wind or the tide, not today of all days.

The creaking sound was coming from inside the lantern room now, where I kept the old storage barrels and maintenance supplies. Curiosity getting the better of me, I pushed open the door and stepped inside.

And that's when I saw it – a small piece of paper floating on the surface of the water in one of the barrels. My heart skipped a beat as I picked up the paper and smoothed out the wrinkles, feeling a shiver run down my spine.

A letter, written in my own handwriting.

James nodded, his expression understanding but guarded. "Of course," he said softly. "Take all the time you need."

To this place? To myself?

It was as if...

The sea itself was telling me something – telling me that I wasn't alone, that there were secrets and stories hidden beneath its surface just waiting to be uncovered.

And I knew, in that moment, what I had to do.

I turned away from the window and walked over to the bookshelf, where a stack of old diaries sat hidden behind a pile of dusty boxes. My father's handwriting leapt out at me as I opened one of them – my mother's voice, telling stories about our family and their connection to the sea.

It was time to uncover the truth.

I blew out the candles, plunging the room into darkness. But the stars outside seemed to shine brighter now, guiding me forward on a journey that felt both terrifying and exhilarating.
